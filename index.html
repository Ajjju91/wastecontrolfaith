Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Industry-standard metrics that drive waste cost governance decisions

Effective waste governance starts with measurement. We track performance using industry-standard KPIs that translate waste data into financial, operational, and sustainability insights. These are the same metrics used by Fortune 500 commercial property managers and waste management leaders.

📊 Total Waste Generated

What it measures: Total tonnage produced per site or portfolio (monthly/annual)

Why it matters: Establishes your baseline and identifies reduction opportunities. Waste volumes often increase 10-20% annually without active governance.

Client value: Enables accurate forecasting, benchmarking across locations, and data-driven reduction targets.

♻️ Waste Diversion Rate

What it measures: Percentage diverted from landfill through recycling, composting, or recovery

Why it matters: A core sustainability metric tied to ESG reporting and increasingly required by investors and regulatory bodies.

Client value: Lower landfill costs + improved environmental performance + enhanced ESG disclosures for stakeholders.

💰 Cost per Tonne Managed

What it measures: True cost efficiency of waste operations (total waste cost ÷ total tonnage)

Why it matters: Reveals hidden pricing inefficiencies, contract issues, and overbilling. Industry benchmarks range $40–$120/tonne depending on waste streams.

Client value: Supports data-driven renegotiation and validates savings month-over-month. Typical improvements: 15–30% reduction.

📞 Service Reliability (On-Time Collection / Missed Pickups)

What it measures: Missed collections, overflow incidents, complaint rates, and vendor SLA compliance

Why it matters: Directly affects site operations, tenant satisfaction, and regulatory compliance. Missed collections lead to overflow incidents and potential fines.

Client value: Improves vendor accountability, reduces operational disruptions, and prevents compliance violations.

⚠️ Invoice Accuracy Rate

What it measures: Billing errors, duplicate charges, unauthorized services, and price deviations vs. contract

Why it matters: Waste invoices are a primary source of cost leakage. Studies show 15–30% of waste invoices contain errors or unauthorized charges.

Client value: Ensures financial transparency and prevents overpayment. Our clients recover 5–15% of annual waste spend through invoice audits alone.

🌍 Carbon & Environmental Impact

What it measures: Carbon emissions linked to waste transport, processing, and disposal (tonnes CO₂ equivalent)

Why it matters: Increasingly required for ESG disclosures, Scope 3 emissions reporting, and investor/stakeholder accountability.

Client value: Supports sustainability reporting, strengthens ESG credentials, and appeals to environmentally conscious stakeholders and tenants.

🚚 Route & Operational Efficiency

What it measures: Collections per route, trucks per week, staff utilization, and frequency optimization

Why it matters: Inefficient routes drive unnecessary cost. Right-sizing frequency and consolidating services often yields immediate savings.

Client value: Reduces operational overhead, improves vendor utilization, and supports sustainable transportation practices.

The Bottom Line: We don't just track waste — we turn data into strategic decisions that reduce costs, improve compliance, and support sustainability goals. Our clients see an average 20–25% cost reduction and 30–40% diversion rate improvement within 12 months.

Understanding Waste Management

Industry knowledge and best practices for commercial waste governance

The Waste Hierarchy

Professional waste strategy follows a globally recognized hierarchy of priorities:

1. Reduce: Minimize waste at the source

2. Reuse: Extend product/material lifespan

3. Recycle: Process materials into new products

4. Recover: Extract energy from waste

5. Dispose: Landfill as last resort

Organizations that follow this hierarchy consistently achieve 30–50% cost reductions and improved ESG performance.

Common Waste Streams

Understanding your waste composition is the foundation of an effective program:

  • General Waste: Mixed refuse destined for landfill or energy recovery
  • Recyclables: Paper, cardboard, glass, metals, plastics
  • Organics: Food waste, green waste, compostable materials
  • Hazardous: Batteries, chemicals, electronics (WEEE), medical waste
  • Specialty: Textiles, construction waste, pallets, scrap metal

Most commercial portfolios waste 15–25% of disposal fees on suboptimal stream management.

5 Best Practices for Waste Cost Governance

1. Establish a Baseline

Conduct a waste audit to understand volumes, waste types, current costs, and vendor performance. You cannot improve what you don't measure.

2. Optimize Services

Review bin sizes, collection frequency, compactor usage, and hauling schedules. Small adjustments often deliver quick savings.

3. Improve Diversion

Implement recycling streams, organics programs, and staff awareness. Diversion reduces landfill fees and supports ESG goals.

4. Strengthen Contracts

Ensure contracts include transparent pricing, clear SLAs, and flexibility for optimization. Vendor governance prevents cost creep.

5. Track KPIs & Review Continuously

Monitor cost per tonne, diversion rate, service reliability, and invoice accuracy. Data drives accountability and uncovers new opportunities.
