Common Questions

Answers about waste audits, cost governance, and contract optimization

About Waste Audits

What exactly happens during a waste audit?

We conduct a detailed 4-6 week audit that includes: a scoping call to understand your portfolio, on-site visits to observe waste operations, invoice reconciliation to identify discrepancies, composition analysis to understand waste types, and a final report with quick wins and a 12-month improvement roadmap.

How long does a waste audit take?

Typically 4-6 weeks from kickoff to final report, depending on portfolio size and complexity. For single-site audits, 2-3 weeks. For multi-site portfolios (8+ locations), allow 6-8 weeks.

What if we have hazardous or special waste streams?

We're fully compliant with EPA hazardous waste regulations and have experience with healthcare waste, electronics recycling, and other specialized streams. We'll coordinate with your vendors on hazard classification and ensure proper handling and documentation.

Will an audit disrupt our operations?

Minimal disruption — we schedule site visits during non-peak times, work with your team to gather data, and keep everything confidential. Most facilities don't even notice we're there.

What happens after the audit report?

You get a written roadmap with 3-5 immediate quick wins (usually cost-neutral or low-cost), medium-term improvements (3-6 months), and longer-term strategic changes. We then work with you to implement, renegotiate contracts, and optimize operations.

About Cost Savings

How much can we typically save?

Most clients see 15-25% cost reduction within the first year from a combination of rate reductions, service right-sizing, consolidation, and billing corrections. Some see 30%+ depending on current vendor relationships and operational inefficiencies.

Are savings guaranteed?

While rare, we don't guarantee savings. However, in our experience, 95%+ of audits identify material opportunities. If opportunities aren't found or are too small to justify change, we'll be honest about it upfront.

What are "quick wins"?

Changes that deliver 5-15% cost reductions with minimal effort or cost to implement: right-sizing bin frequencies, removing duplicate services, consolidating vendors, fixing billing errors, or improving recycling rates without capital spend.

How are savings tracked and verified?

We establish a baseline from your audit, document each implemented change, and track results monthly via invoice analysis and KPI reporting. Savings are measured in dollars, tonnage, and diversion rate improvements.

What if our vendors refuse to negotiate?

We help you evaluate alternatives. Often the threat of RFP or switching is enough — vendors know the cost of losing you. In some markets, competitive options are limited; we'll advise on realistic expectations and timing.

About Vendor Management & Contracts

Can you help us renegotiate existing contracts?

Yes — contract review and renegotiation is one of our core services. We analyze terms, benchmark against market rates, identify leverage points, and negotiate new rates or improved terms on your behalf.

How often should we re-bid waste contracts?

Every 2-3 years minimum, or whenever major operational changes occur. Market rates fluctuate; regular competitive bids keep you from overpaying and remind vendors you have options.

What's included in vendor performance reviews?

Service delivery KPIs (missed pickups, missed compactions), billing accuracy, timeliness of reports, compliance with special requests, sustainability performance (recycling rates, diversion metrics), and cost competitiveness vs. market rates.

Can you help consolidate vendors?

Absolutely — vendor consolidation often delivers 10-15% savings. We evaluate the pros/cons, help negotiate terms with consolidated vendors, and manage transition logistics across your portfolio.

What about single vs. multi-vendor strategies?

It depends on your portfolio size and locations. Single vendors (like Waste Management, Republic) simplify invoicing and relationships but can limit negotiating power. Multi-vendor approaches can be cheaper but more complex. We recommend the optimal strategy for your situation.

About Sustainability & Compliance

How does waste governance align with ESG goals?

Modern waste governance combines cost reduction with sustainability: increasing landfill diversion rates, reducing overall waste tonnage, optimizing recycling, and tracking Scope 3 emissions. You can improve both bottom line and ESG metrics simultaneously.

Can we measure and report waste metrics?

Yes — our reporting includes diversion rates, waste per square foot, cost per tonne, recycling percentages, and emissions equivalents. These metrics feed into ESG reports, sustainability goals, and board-level communication.

Are there compliance risks we should know about?

Primarily hazardous waste handling (healthcare, electronics, construction), proper documentation, and chain of custody for certain waste streams. We ensure compliance with EPA and state regulations; it's part of what we verify during audits.

How do you handle confidentiality with sensitive waste data?

All audit data and findings are strictly confidential and covered by our engagement agreement. We never share client information with competitors or vendors without explicit permission.

About Engagement & Process

How do we get started?

Schedule a 30-minute discovery call to discuss your portfolio, current challenges, and goals. If it looks like a good fit, we'll prepare a proposal with scope, timeline, and pricing. Engagements typically start within 1-2 weeks of agreement.

What's the typical project timeline?

Audit: 4-6 weeks. Implementation of quick wins: 1-3 months. Contract renegotiation: 2-4 months. Retainer governance: ongoing, starting month 1 of engagement.

Do we need to be an existing customer with a waste vendor?

Not necessarily — we can help if you're setting up waste services for the first time, switching vendors, or optimizing existing relationships. The audit is valuable in all scenarios.

What support do you provide after the audit?

That depends on your engagement model: Fixed-price audits include a roadmap and general guidance. Retainer agreements include ongoing invoice review, KPI reporting, and quarterly reviews. Implementation support is available à la carte.

Can we test this with a single site first?

Absolutely — many large portfolios start with a pilot site to prove value, then expand. A single-site audit takes 2-3 weeks and helps build business case for portfolio-wide engagement.

What if we're in between vendors or going through M&A?

Perfect timing for an audit. M&A, consolidations, and vendor transitions are ideal moments to optimize. We often accelerate audits during these periods because the leverage for change is highest.
